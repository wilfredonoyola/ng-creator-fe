Every clip comes out in 1080p with the same frames per second as the original: the video you uploaded isn't compressed again. Clipfine also warns you when something lowers the quality. All of this is available on the web and in the app.

## The quality indicator

1. At the top of the editor you'll see the **Quality: Excellent**, **Good** or **Low** badge. While you edit, it's an estimate based on the current framing.
2. Hover over it or tap it to see the details: the source resolution and how much the image is upscaled. After you export, the quality is measured from the MP4.
3. If it says **Low**, enlarge the framing box in **Format** so the image is upscaled less, or use a higher-resolution source.

## See how it looks on each network

1. Tap the phone button next to play: **Preview as TikTok, Reels or Shorts**.
2. Pick the network to lay its screen over the clip and check that no text or caption is hidden behind its buttons.

It only works when the clip is 9:16.

## Clips longer than 90 seconds on Facebook

Facebook Reels last up to 90 seconds. If your clip is longer, the editor lets you know.

1. When scheduling, open **Facebook version (up to 90 s)**.
2. Drag the segment's **Start** and **End** and check the **Segment preview**.
3. Tap **Process short version**. The other networks get the whole clip.

To download the finished MP4, see [Export and download](/ayuda/exportar). To choose networks and times, see [Scheduling and calendar](/ayuda/programar-y-calendario).

> **Tip:** check the quality badge after using **Auto-frame** or changing the aspect ratio: a very tight frame upscales the image and can drop it to **Good** or **Low**.
