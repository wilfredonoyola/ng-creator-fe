Podcasts are shot in landscape with several people on camera. To make sure the vertical clip always shows whoever is talking, Clipfine has auto-framing and screen layouts built for interviews.

## Auto-framing

Auto-framing recognizes voices, watches who is moving their lips, and builds the clip's framing changes on its own.

1. Open the clip in the editor and pick the **Auto-frame** tool.
2. Click **Auto-frame**. You'll see each stage (finding faces, listening to who speaks, building the framing) and how long is left.
3. While it works, you can keep editing text and captions: the framing arrives on its own.
4. When it's done, it tells you how many people it found and how many framing changes it made. Use the button to jump to the first change and check it.

If you don't like the result, **Go back to how it was before auto-framing** restores the clip. Keep in mind that auto-framing again replaces any framing changes you made by hand.

In the app you can also turn it on when you create the clip.

## Layouts and manual framing

In the **Format** tool you choose:

- **Aspect ratio**: 9:16 for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, 1:1 for the feed, or 16:9 for YouTube.
- The **layout** for each segment: **Single** (one frame), **Split** (two people, one above the other), **Landscape** (the full 16:9, centered) or **Centered** (a square in the middle).
- **Background**: **Blurred** or **Black**, when the video doesn't fill the screen.

To fix something, drag the box over the video to move it and use the corner to resize it. **Framing here** adds a framing change from the second you're on.

All of this is available on the web and in the app. For the rest of the tools, see [The clip editor](/ayuda/editor-basico).

> **Tip:** the editor's quality badge tells you whether the clip will look **Excellent**, **Good** or **Low**. If it drops, zoom the box out or ask the client for a higher-resolution recording.
